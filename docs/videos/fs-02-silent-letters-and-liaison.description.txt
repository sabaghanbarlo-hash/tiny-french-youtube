GRAMMAR
Final consonants are usually silent - In French the last consonant of a word is often not pronounced: petit, grand, beaucoup, très, vous, trop. Read the word without that last letter and you are usually right.
The exceptions: c, r, f, l - The letters in the word CaReFuL are often pronounced at the end of a word: sac, bonjour, neuf, hôtel. This is a handy rule of thumb, not a law, so keep listening to real French.
Liaison - A silent final consonant can come back when the next word starts with a vowel sound. That link is called liaison. S and x sound like z, n stays n and t stays t.
