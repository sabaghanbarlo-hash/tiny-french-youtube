[grammar]
grammar: What is a nasal vowel? | In a nasal vowel the air goes through your nose and your mouth at the same time. The letter n or m after the vowel is not pronounced as a separate n. Say "bon" and the n disappears: you only hear a nasal "oh".
example: bon | nasal “oh” — BOHN
example: pain | nasal “a” — PAN
grammar: Three main sounds | An and en sound like a nasal "ah". On sounds like a nasal "oh". In, ain and ein sound like a nasal "a" as in "can". Un is close to in.
example: un enfant | a child
example: demain | tomorrow
example: un garçon | a boy
grammar: When the n is NOT nasal | If the n is doubled or followed by a vowel, the vowel is not nasal and the n is pronounced: bon but bonne; un but une. That difference often marks masculine and feminine.
example: bon / bonne | BOHN / BUN
example: un / une | uhn / ewn
