[story]
Marinette: Comment ça s'écrit, Adrien ? | How do you spell that, Adrien?
Adrien: A, D, R, I, E, N. | A, D, R, I, E, N.
Marinette: Et ton nom de famille ? | And your surname?
Adrien: Martin. M, A, R, T, I, N. | Martin. M, A, R, T, I, N.
Adrien: Et toi ? Comment ça s'écrit, Marinette ? | And you? How do you spell Marinette?
Marinette: M, A, R, I, N, E, T, T, E. Il y a deux T. | M, A, R, I, N, E, T, T, E. There are two Ts.
Adrien: Il y a un accent ? | Is there an accent?
Marinette: Non. Mais « café » s'écrit avec un é : un accent aigu. | No. But “café” is written with an é: an acute accent.

[grammar]
grammar: The French letter names | Every letter has a French name that is different from English. Listen, and repeat after me. The best practice is to spell your own name out loud.
example: A, B, C, D, E, F, G | The first seven letters
example: A, D, R, I, E, N | Spelling Adrien
grammar: What accents do | Accents change how a letter sounds. É sounds like "ay". È and ê sound like "eh". Ç sounds like "s" before a, o or u. The accent on à only separates words: a and à sound the same, as do ou and où.
example: un café | a coffee — say “kah-FAY”
example: un garçon | a boy — say “gar-SOHN”
grammar: Double letters | When spelling, say deux before a double letter: deux T. The doubled consonant is still pronounced just once in normal speech.
example: Il y a deux T. | There are two Ts.
