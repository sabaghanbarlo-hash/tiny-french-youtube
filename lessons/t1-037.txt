title: Tiny French 1000 · Lesson 37 · Conversation connectors and fillers
episode: T1-037
scene: classroom

[story]
Adrien: Salut, Marinette ! En fait, le café est fermé. | Hi, Marinette! Actually, the café is closed.
Marinette: Ah bon ? Euh, on fait quoi ? | Oh really? Um, what should we do?
Adrien: Ben, on peut prendre le bus, du coup. | Well, in that case, we can take the bus.
Marinette: D'accord. Sinon, on va au parc ? | Okay. Alternatively, shall we go to the park?
Adrien: Voilà, bonne idée ! Le parc est près d'ici. | Exactly, good idea! The park is nearby.
Marinette: D'ailleurs, tu as vu le nouveau café ? C'est genre juste à côté. | By the way, have you seen the new café? It's, like, right next door.
Adrien: Enfin, je préfère le parc. Il fait beau. | Well, I prefer the park. The weather is nice.
Marinette: Bref, allons-y ! | Anyway, let's go!

[vocab]
vocab: en fait | actually; in fact | ahn FEH
example: En fait, le plan change. | Actually, the plan is changing.
vocab: du coup | so; as a result | dew KOO
example: Du coup, la situation évolue. | So, the situation is changing.
vocab: enfin | well; finally | ahn-FAN
example: Enfin, le débat continue. | Well, the debate continues.
vocab: bref | in short; anyway | BREHF
example: Bref, la réunion se termine. | Anyway, the meeting is ending.
vocab: voilà | there; there you go | vwah-LAH
example: Voilà, le résultat final. | There you go, the final result.
vocab: euh | um; uh | UH
example: Euh, une seconde. | Um, one second.
vocab: ben | well | BEHN
example: Ben, chacun choisit. | Well, everyone chooses.
vocab: d'ailleurs | besides; by the way | dah-YUHR
example: D'ailleurs, tu as raison. | Besides, you’re right.
vocab: sinon | otherwise | see-NOHN
example: Sinon, on prend le bus ? | Otherwise, shall we take the bus?
vocab: genre | like (filler) | ZHAHN-ruh
example: Il était, genre, vraiment sympa. | He was, like, really nice.

[grammar]
grammar: Connectors in a sentence | Conversation connectors can appear at the beginning or end of a sentence, or between two ideas. A comma often marks a short pause after a connector at the beginning or before one at the end.
example: En fait, le café est fermé. | Actually, the café is closed.
example: On peut prendre le bus, du coup. | We can take the bus, in that case.

[quiz]
quiz: What does « du coup » mean here? | As a result | By the way | In short | A | Adrien suggests taking the bus as a result of the café being closed.
quiz: Which word can mean « um » when you are thinking? | Voilà | Euh | Sinon | B | « Euh » is a common French hesitation sound, like « um ».
quiz: What does “genre” mean? | there | so | like (filler) | C | genre means “like (filler)”.
quiz: What does “du coup” mean? | in short | otherwise | so | C | du coup means “so”.
quiz: What does “sous” mean? | so | without | under | C | sous means “under”.
