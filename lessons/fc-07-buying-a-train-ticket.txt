title: Buying a Train Ticket
episode: FC7
scene: station

[story]
Adrien: Bonjour, madame. Je voudrais un billet pour Lyon, s'il vous plaît. | Hello, madam. I would like a ticket to Lyon, please.
Marinette: Un aller simple ou un aller-retour ? | A one-way or a return?
Adrien: Un aller-retour, s'il vous plaît. | A return, please.
Marinette: Pour quel jour ? | For which day?
Adrien: Pour demain, le matin. | For tomorrow, in the morning.
Marinette: Il y a un train à neuf heures. Ça vous va ? | There's a train at nine o'clock. Does that suit you?
Adrien: Oui, parfait. C'est combien ? | Yes, perfect. How much is it?
Marinette: Vingt euros. | Twenty euros.
Adrien: Voilà. De quel quai part le train ? | Here you go. Which platform does the train leave from?
Marinette: Quai numéro trois. Bon voyage ! | Platform number three. Have a good trip!

[grammar]
grammar: Un billet pour + place | Use un billet pour and the name of the place to ask for a ticket. The word pour means "for" or "to" here.
example: Un billet pour Lyon. | A ticket to Lyon.
example: Deux billets pour Paris. | Two tickets to Paris.
grammar: Aller simple or aller-retour? | An aller simple is one way. An aller-retour is a return ticket, literally "go and back". Staff nearly always ask this question first.
example: Un aller simple, s'il vous plaît. | A one-way ticket, please.
example: Un aller-retour pour Nice. | A return ticket to Nice.
grammar: Quel + noun | Quel means "which" or "what". It agrees with the noun: quel jour, quel quai, but quelle heure and quelle gare. It always comes before the noun.
example: Quel quai ? | Which platform?
example: Quelle heure ? | What time?
