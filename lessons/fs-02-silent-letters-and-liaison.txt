title: Silent Letters and Liaison
episode: FS2
scene: restaurant

[story]
Marinette: Bonjour ! Vous êtes combien ? | Hello! How many are you?
Adrien: Nous sommes trois amis. | There are three of us friends.
Marinette: Entrez, les amis ! Il y a une table ici. | Come in, friends! There's a table here.
Adrien: Merci. Nous avons très faim ! | Thanks. We're very hungry!
Marinette: Vous avez de la chance. La cuisine est ouverte. | You're lucky. The kitchen is open.

[grammar]
grammar: Final consonants are usually silent | In French the last consonant of a word is often not pronounced: petit, grand, beaucoup, très, vous, trop. Read the word without that last letter and you are usually right.
example: petit | say “puh-TEE”, not “puh-TEET”
example: très | say “TREH”
grammar: The exceptions: c, r, f, l | The letters in the word CaReFuL are often pronounced at the end of a word: sac, bonjour, neuf, hôtel. This is a handy rule of thumb, not a law, so keep listening to real French.
example: bonjour | the r is pronounced
example: un hôtel | the l is pronounced
grammar: Liaison | A silent final consonant can come back when the next word starts with a vowel sound. That link is called liaison. S and x sound like z, n stays n and t stays t.
example: les amis | lay zah-MEE
example: vous avez | voo zah-VAY
