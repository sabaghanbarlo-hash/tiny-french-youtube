title: Tiny French 1000 · Lesson 9 · Numbers 1 to 10
episode: T1-009

[story]
Adrien: On compte les marches ? Un, deux, trois. | Shall we count the steps? One, two, three.
Marinette: Oui ! Quatre, cinq, six. | Yes! Four, five, six.
Adrien: Sept, huit, neuf... | Seven, eight, nine...
Marinette: Et dix ! Voilà, dix marches. | And ten! There we go, ten steps.
Adrien: C'était facile, non ? | That was easy, wasn't it?
Marinette: Oui, comptons encore ! | Yes, let's count again!
Adrien: Un, deux, trois ! | One, two, three!
Marinette: Merci, Adrien ! | Thank you, Adrien!

[vocab]
vocab: un | one | UH(n)
example: J'ai un frère. | I have one brother.
vocab: deux | two | DUH
example: Elle a deux chats. | She has two cats.
vocab: trois | three | TRWAH
example: Nous avons trois pommes. | We have three apples.
vocab: quatre | four | KATR
example: Il y a quatre livres. | There are four books.
vocab: cinq | five | SANK
example: J'ai cinq euros. | I have five euros.
vocab: six | six | SEESS
example: Les six enfants jouent. | The six children are playing.
vocab: sept | seven | SET
example: Il y a sept jours. | There are seven days.
vocab: huit | eight | WEET
example: Huit heures, c'est tôt. | Eight o'clock is early.
vocab: neuf | nine | NUHF
example: Il a neuf ans. | He is nine years old.
vocab: dix | ten | DEESS
example: Dix élèves arrivent. | Ten students are arriving.

[grammar]
grammar: Numbers before nouns | In French, a number usually comes before the noun it counts, as in dix marches (ten steps). When counting aloud, you can also say the numbers on their own.
example: Il y a trois marches. | There are three steps.
example: Je vois cinq vélos. | I see five bicycles.

[quiz]
