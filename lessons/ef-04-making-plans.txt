title: Making Plans
episode: EF4

[story]
Marinette: Tu es libre samedi ? | Are you free on Saturday?
Adrien: Oui, pourquoi ? | Yes, why?
Marinette: On va au cinéma ? | Shall we go to the cinema?
Adrien: Bonne idée ! À quelle heure ? | Good idea! At what time?
Marinette: À sept heures. | At seven.
Adrien: D'accord. On se retrouve où ? | OK. Where shall we meet?
Marinette: Devant le cinéma. | In front of the cinema.
Adrien: Parfait. Je vais acheter les billets. | Perfect. I'll buy the tickets.
Marinette: Super ! À samedi ! | Great! See you Saturday!

[grammar]
grammar: The verb aller (to go) | Aller is irregular, so learn it by heart: je vais, tu vas, il / elle / on va, nous allons, vous allez, ils / elles vont. On is the everyday word for "we" and uses the same form as il.
example: Je vais au cinéma. | I'm going to the cinema.
example: Tu vas à Paris. | You're going to Paris.
example: On va au café. | We're going to the café.
grammar: à + le = au | Use à to mean "to" or "at". With le it becomes au: au cinéma, au café. With la it stays à la: à la plage. With les it becomes aux.
example: au cinéma | to the cinema
example: à la plage | to the beach
grammar: Aller + infinitive = near future | Je vais + another verb means "I'm going to…". It is the easiest way to talk about the future: je vais acheter, on va manger.
example: Je vais acheter les billets. | I'm going to buy the tickets.
example: On va manger à sept heures. | We're going to eat at seven.

[quiz]
quiz: What does “On va au cinéma ?” mean? | Shall we go to the cinema? | Are you at the cinema? | I love the cinema. | A | On va… ? suggests going somewhere together.
quiz: Which word means “OK”? | Devant | D'accord | Surtout | B | D'accord = OK / agreed.
quiz: What does “devant” mean? | Next to | Behind | In front of | C | Devant le cinéma = in front of the cinema.
quiz: à + le becomes… | au | al | à le | A | Au cinéma, au café.
